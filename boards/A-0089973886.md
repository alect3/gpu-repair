# Board A

- Model: Zotac RTX 3090 Trinity
- Serial: 0089973886
- Status: dead, no power at all

## Symptoms

- No power at all when booted. No fans, no lights, no POST.
- The PC itself ran fine with the card installed. The PSU did not trip, so
  the card is not shorting under power. It is simply not enabling.

## Findings

- Resistance to ground is normal on every rail: 12V, 5V, 3.3V, 1.8V, PEX,
  core and memory
  (measured 2026-09-05).
- Two capacitors and a resistor had fallen off (same as Board B, see
  [common.md](../common.md)). Re-soldered before testing began. 5V reads
  normal to ground, so the re-fit on this board did not introduce a short.
- 12V input path from the 8-pin connectors to the VRM input is intact
  (continuity, 2026-09-05).
- The 5V regulator on the back, directly behind the re-fitted parts, gets
  warm when powered while its output stays at 0V. A buck IC that heats
  with no output is either internally damaged or driving into a short and
  hiccuping.
- Working theory now: same root cause as Board B. The impact that knocked
  the parts off damaged the 5V regulator area on both boards. The earlier
  "5V resistance normal" reading needs re-checking at this inductor.

  Board B: A fails dead with a hot regulator, B fails short.

  Board B. So the 5V circuit is shorted on this board too, matching the
  warm regulator IC.

## Next steps

The 5V regulator behind the re-fitted parts is the focus. Its IC warms up
with 0V out, and every other rail hangs off 5V or the sequencing it gates.

Unpowered first. The caps read 0 ohm, so the 5V net is shorted here too:

1. Lift one end of each re-fitted cap in turn. Measure the lifted cap on
   its own and the pads on their own.
   - Cap reads 0 ohm alone: cracked. Replace with new.
   - Pads still 0 ohm with cap lifted: the short is elsewhere on the net.
2. If both caps are lifted and the 5V net still reads 0 ohm to ground, the
   short is most likely the regulator IC on the back (it runs warm).
   Remove it and re-measure the net.
3. Resistance to ground at the regulator IC's input pin on the back, to
   make sure the short has not spread to the 12V side.

Then powered, on the back-side IC:

4. Read the IC marking. Look up the pinout.
5. Input (expect 12V), enable, and output (the inductor).
   - Output shorted to ground unpowered: find the short. Prime suspects are
     the re-fitted caps (cracked MLCC) and damaged pads or traces under
     them.
   - Output not shorted, IC warm, 0V out: IC is damaged. Replace it.

6. Once 5V is back, re-check 1.8V, PEX, core and memory. They may all
   simply follow.
7. Top left GS9216 (0V) is on hold. Very likely it is just waiting on 5V or
   the sequencer. Revisit only if it stays dead after 5V is fixed.
